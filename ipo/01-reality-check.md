# 1. Reality check and strategy (Prompt 1)

Evidence snapshot: **2 October 2026**. Lines marked **(E)** are my scenario estimates, not reported data. Everything else is sourced at the bottom of the page. Re-check rates and rules before acting.

## Terms you'll meet (defined once here)

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **IPO** | A company sells shares to the public for the first time, then lists on NSE/BSE. |
| **Mainboard vs SME** | Mainboard is the normal NSE/BSE market. SME is a separate platform (NSE Emerge, BSE SME) for small companies, with looser rules and far bigger minimum tickets. |
| **DRHP / RHP** | Draft / final Red Herring Prospectus: the offer document filed with SEBI. The RHP adds the price band and dates. |
| **Price band, cut-off** | The price range you can bid in. Retail bidders can tick **cut-off**, which means "I accept the final price", nearly always the top of the band. |
| **Lot size** | The minimum number of shares in one bid. You bid in multiples of it. |
| **Fresh issue / OFS** | Fresh issue: new shares, and the money goes to the company. OFS (offer for sale): existing owners sell their shares, and the money goes to them. |
| **QIB / NII / RII** | QIB: Qualified Institutional Buyers (mutual funds, insurers, banks, foreign funds). NII: Non-Institutional Investors, i.e. individuals bidding above ₹2 lakh; small NII (sNII) is ₹2–10 lakh, big NII (bNII) is above ₹10 lakh. RII: Retail Individual Investors, bidding up to ₹2 lakh. That's you. |
| **Anchor investors** | Big QIBs who are allotted shares the working day before the issue opens. Their shares are locked in for 30 days (half) and 90 days (the rest). |
| **ASBA / UPI mandate** | ASBA is *Application Supported by Blocked Amount*: your money stays in your bank account and is only **blocked**. When you bid through a broker app, the block is created by a **UPI mandate** that you approve with your UPI PIN. |
| **Subscription (x)** | Shares bid ÷ shares offered in a category. "10x" means ten times oversubscribed. |
| **Basis of allotment** | The registrar's final allocation, published the day after the issue closes. |
| **T+3** | T is the closing day. Allotment is on T+1, shares are credited and unused money unblocked on T+2, and the stock lists on T+3 (all working days). |
| **GMP** | Grey Market Premium: an unofficial, unregulated price quoted for IPO shares before listing. |
| **Listing gain** | (Listing price − issue price) ÷ issue price. |
| **Pre-open session** | On listing day, a 9:00–10:00 call auction discovers the opening price. Normal trading starts at 10:00. |

## Bottom line first

- **Listing gains are real but lumpy.** In April–September 2026, India had a record ₹94,205 crore of mainboard IPOs and an average listing gain of 19%. A year earlier the average was 7%. In FY2024-25 it was 29%. These averages are pulled up by a few big winners.
- **You rarely get the IPOs that pop.** Retail allotment is a lottery. In hot issues your odds are often 1–10%. In issues nobody wants you get full allotment, and those are the ones that list flat or below the issue price.
- **SME IPOs are out for now.** The minimum application is two lots, typically ₹2.4–2.9 lakh. Their 2026 *median* return is about 4%, against a headline *average* of about 24%.
- **The rupee upside is small (E).** With 1-lot bids (~₹15,000), a realistic year runs from a small loss to a few thousand rupees of gain, with a good year near ₹25,000. Treat it as a paid learning lab with hard limits.

## How an IPO actually works

1. **Filing.** The company files a DRHP with SEBI. SEBI issues "observations", which amount to clearance. The company then files the RHP, announces the price band, and books anchors one working day before opening.
2. **Bidding.** Mainboard issues stay open for 3 working days; SME issues usually 3–5. Institutions and HNIs (high-net-worth individuals) bid heavily on the last afternoon, so the day-3 numbers matter most.
3. **T+3.** T+1 allotment, T+2 shares credited and money unblocked, T+3 listing. This has been mandatory since 1 December 2023.
4. **Listing day.** The pre-open auction sets the opening price. If the issue was ₹250 crore or less, the stock trades with a ±5% price band and in the trade-for-trade segment (every trade must be settled by delivery, so no intraday) for its first 10 days. Larger issues get a 20% band.

| | Mainboard | SME (NSE Emerge, BSE SME) |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum application | One lot, which SEBI requires to be in the ₹10,000–15,000 range | **Two lots**: Eventions (open now) needs ₹2,83,200 |
| Who can list | A profit track-record route ("Reg 6(1)") or a QIB-heavy route for companies without one ("Reg 6(2)") | Since March 2025: ≥₹1 crore operating profit in 2 of the last 3 years, OFS ≤20% of the issue |
| Listing day | 5% band + trade-for-trade if ≤₹250 crore, else 20% | NSE caps the opening price at 90% above issue; thin trading in lots |
| Typical 2026 outcome | Average listing gain 19% (April–September) | Average return 24.4% but **median 3.9%**; only about half trade above issue |

### How listing gains happen

A stock pops when institutions and HNIs who missed out at the issue price buy on day one. Oversubscription measures how much demand went unfilled. In the six consecutive mainboard listings of 30 September and 1 October 2026, final-day demand was the best single guide:

| IPO (listing date) | Size | Final subscription: total / QIB / NII / retail | Opened vs issue price (NSE) | Rest of listing day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adroit Industries (30 Sep) | ₹151 cr | 175.6x / 195.0x / 331.9x / 97.6x | **+75%** | Locked at the 5% upper circuit, ~+85% |
| Moneyview (1 Oct) | ₹1,092 cr | 101.9x / 230.5x / 120.4x / 20.4x | **+62%** | High +82%, closed ~+59% |
| A-One Steels (1 Oct) | ₹405 cr | 12.2x / 7.7x / 25.8x / 9.1x | **+12.4%** | Faded; closed **+2.9%** |
| Swastika Infra (30 Sep) | ₹161 cr | 7.8x / 3.3x / 19.3x / 5.4x | +8% | Traded ~+13% |
| ArMee Infotech (30 Sep) | ₹300 cr | 2.6x / 3.2x / 2.2x / 2.6x | 0% | Fell to **−15%** intraday |
| Elevate Campuses (30 Sep) | ₹2,100 cr | 1.9x / 2.7x / 0.9x / 0.9x | **−1.9%** | — |
| *NSE Ltd (24 Sep)* | ₹22,562 cr, all OFS | 5.7x / 12.7x / 6.5x / 1.4x | +0.8% (BSE) | — |

Six listings are a tiny sample, but the pattern matches longer data. Hot books pop; lukewarm books list flat or lower. Even a decent pop can fade within hours: A-One gave back three-quarters of its opening gain by the close.

## Who gets shares, and why allotment is never guaranteed

| Category (book-built issue) | Reg 6(1) issue: profitable track record | Reg 6(2) issue: no track record |
|---|---|---|
| QIB | ≤50%, of which up to 60% can go to anchors. Within the anchor book, 40% is now reserved: 33% for mutual funds, 7% for insurers and pension funds | ≥75% |
| NII | ≥15% (one-third sNII, two-thirds bNII) | ≤15% |
| Retail | **≥35%** (SEBI dropped its 2025 idea of cutting this for large IPOs) | **≤10%** |

**The retail lottery.** If retail is oversubscribed, the registrar works out how many minimum lots are available (retail shares ÷ lot size). It then draws lots so each winning applicant gets one lot. Your odds are therefore *at least* 1 ÷ (retail subscription), and higher when many people bid for several lots.

- **A-One Steels:** 34.8 lakh retail shares ÷ 37 = 94,079 lots. Retail was 9.09x, so each applicant had at least an **11%** chance.
- **Adroit:** retail 97.6x → at least ~1%.
- **Moneyview:** retail 20.4x → at least ~5%.
- **NSE Ltd:** retail 1.36x → at least ~74%, possibly everyone. But it listed only +0.8%.

**Big, boring issues give high odds and small pops. Small, hot issues give big pops and terrible odds.** Bidding 13 lots instead of 1 does **not** raise your retail odds; it just blocks more money. The small-NII category (₹2–10 lakh) is also a lottery, at its minimum application size.

**Why it can fail even when you "applied":**
- you didn't approve the UPI mandate by **5 PM on the closing day**;
- the UPI ID or bank account isn't in your own name;
- there was a PAN/demat mismatch;
- you made two bids on one PAN in the same category, so **both get rejected**;
- the bank or UPI app dropped the mandate;
- the issue itself failed (for example, a Reg 6(2) issue that can't fill its 75% QIB quota refunds everyone; verify in the specific RHP).

## Returns and losses, 2024–2026

| Period | Mainboard IPOs | Money raised | Average listing-day gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calendar 2024 | 91 | ₹1.59 lakh cr | ~30% |
| Calendar 2025 | 103 | ₹1.75 lakh cr (record) | **~9%** |
| FY2024-25 | 78 | ₹1.62 lakh cr | 29% |

| Apr–Sep 2026 (H1 FY27) | 78 | ₹94,205 cr (record half-year) | **19%**; 46 of 64 listed (72%) were above issue price on 29 Sep |

- **Size mattered in 2025:** IPOs above ₹5,000 crore averaged 22% at listing, against 7.5% for those below ₹1,000 crore.
- **September 2026:** 32 mainboard listings; 21 were in profit and 11 in loss by early October.
- **SME:**
  - Since 2020, about 73% listed at a premium (963 issues to June 2026).
  - Of the 126 SME IPOs tracked in 2026, 21 became multibaggers (doubled or better), but only 65 trade above the offer price. The average return of 24.4% hides a **3.9% median**.
- **The backdrop is shaky:**
  - The Nifty fell **6.1% in September 2026** to 22,620, its worst September since 2018, and is down roughly 13–14% for the year.
  - Foreign investors pulled out about ₹36,000 crore in September.
  - The IPO boom is running against a falling secondary market. If that continues, listing gains usually shrink.

**Loss scenarios to expect:**
- Listing below issue price (Elevate −1.9%).
- A flat open that collapses intraday (ArMee −15%).
- A pop that fades before you act (A-One).
- Full allotment in exactly the weak issues (adverse selection).
- Locked circuits that stop you selling on small issues.
- In SME, being unable to exit at all for days.
- Scams: Telegram "allotment guaranteed" groups, fake apps, grey-market "kostak" deals. Kostak means selling your application in advance for a fixed price; it is unregulated and you have no recourse.

## How much capital you need

- **One lot ≈ ₹15,000.** Today's open issues need ₹14,960 (Vishal Nirmiti) and ₹15,000 (Nityas Gems).
- **The retail cap is ₹2 lakh per IPO**, but bidding more lots doesn't help, as shown above.
- **Money is blocked, not spent.** It is blocked for about 5 working days (bid → T+2), it keeps earning savings interest, and it comes back if you aren't allotted.
- **A ₹30,000–45,000 pool** lets you have 2–3 applications open at once. Busy weeks overlap: four mainboard issues closed on 29 September alone.
- **SME:** ₹2.4–2.9 lakh per application → excluded by your risk limits.
- **Jio Platforms** (pending; SEBI cleared it on 28 Aug 2026; about ₹37,700 crore; dates not announced):
  - A 35% retail book that size means millions of one-lot allotments, so odds should be far better than in small hot issues. NSE Ltd is a reminder that big issues can list flat.
  - The DRHP mentions a reservation for eligible Reliance shareholders. That is a *separate category set out in the RHP*, not a second application in the same category. Check the record date in the RHP, and don't buy RIL just to qualify unless you'd hold it anyway.

## Tax: listing-day gains vs holding (verify; details in [07-money-plan.md](07-money-plan.md))

- **Sell within 12 months:** short-term capital gains (STCG), taxed at **20% + 4% cess**. This is s.196 of the Income-tax Act 2025, which replaced s.111A from 1 April 2026; Budget 2026 left the rate unchanged.
- **Hold more than 12 months:** long-term capital gains (LTCG), taxed at **12.5%** on gains above **₹1.25 lakh a year** (s.198, formerly s.112A).
- **The ₹12 lakh "zero tax" rebate (s.87A) does not cover STCG** from FY2025-26 onward.
- **Unused basic exemption** (₹4 lakh under the new regime) *can* absorb STCG if your other taxable income is below it.
- **STT** (securities transaction tax) is 0.1% of the sale value. Budget 2026 raised STT only on futures and options.
- **Worked example (A-One, 1 lot):**
  - You buy 37 × ₹405 = ₹14,985 and sell at the ₹455 open = ₹16,835, a gain of ₹1,850.
  - Selling costs are about ₹35 (STT ~₹17, DP charge ~₹15, small exchange fees).
  - Tax at 20.8% is about ₹380, unless basic-exemption room covers it.
  - **You keep about ₹1,435–1,815.**
- **Frequent flipping** can be argued to be business income (CBDT circular 6/2016). For a handful of IPOs a year it is normally STCG; ask a CA if your volume grows.

## A sensible beginner strategy

1. **Paper-run for two weeks first.** Use the routine (file 06) and log every decision with its outcome.
2. **Mainboard, retail category, 1 lot, at cut-off.** No SME, no bids above ₹2 lakh, no "HNI" bidding.
3. **Apply only to IPOs that clear the scorecard** ([04-evaluation-scorecard.md](04-evaluation-scorecard.md)): no knock-outs, a score of 14/20 or more, and passing the demand gate.
4. **Decide on the closing day after about 2 PM**, when the institutional and HNI numbers show. Bid, then approve the mandate immediately; the deadline is 5 PM, so aim to finish by 3 PM.
5. **Sizing:** ₹15,000 per IPO, at most 2 open applications (₹30,000 blocked), at most 6 applications a month. These are the defaults in `risk-limits.yaml`; raise them only after 3 months of logged results.
6. **Write your listing-day plan before listing:**
   - Opens above issue → sell within the first 30 minutes of normal trading.
   - Locked at the upper circuit → hold until it unlocks.
   - Opens below issue → exit that day, because the listing-gain thesis failed.
   - Never average down.
7. **Hold instead of selling only if** you'd happily *buy* it at the listing price and own it for 12+ months. That is a different decision from flipping.
8. **Stop rules:**
   - 3 losing allotments in a row → one month paper-only.
   - Realised loss above ₹3,000 in a quarter → paper-only for the rest of the quarter.

## Common beginner mistakes

- Bidding the maximum number of retail lots "to improve odds" (it doesn't).
- Two applications on one PAN in the same category (both rejected).
- Using a family member's UPI ID (rejected; it must be your own account).
- Trusting GMP sites or Telegram tips over the order book.
- Forgetting to approve the mandate.
- Not setting up TPIN/DDPI, so the sell order fails at 10:00 on listing day. (TPIN is the depository PIN that authorises a sale; DDPI is a one-time authorisation so you don't need it each time.)
- Holding a loser "until it comes back".
- Chasing SME issues with borrowed or emergency money.
- Applying without opening the RHP.
- Ignoring tax and ITR reporting.

## What "daily IPO filing" means in practice

- **Volume:** India lists about 100 mainboard IPOs a year (91 in 2024, 103 in 2025), and they arrive in bunches. April–July 2026 was quiet; September alone had 32 listings.
- **Applications:** expect 2–8 in a busy month and zero in a quiet one, about 5 minutes each.
- **What's daily:** the **research digest**, about 3 minutes. SME issues open almost daily, but your rules filter them out.

## What you can and cannot expect

**You can expect (E):**
- Allotment in only a minority of the hot IPOs you bid for (often 1–15% per application), but near-certain allotment in weak ones.
- Allotted lots in hot issues making roughly +₹1,500 to +₹12,000 (+10% to +80%), and weak ones −₹2,000 to +₹750.
- Over a year, on a ₹15,000–45,000 pool, a net result of roughly **−₹5,000 to +₹25,000** before tax, highly dependent on the market's mood. For comparison, ₹30,000 in a 6.5% FD earns about ₹2,000 with zero effort.
- A genuine education in reading prospectuses, valuation and market plumbing. That is the main return.

**You cannot expect:**
- Monthly income, or any guaranteed listing gain.
- Bigger retail bids to scale your profits.
- To reach the ~₹10 lakh+ you'll need to show for Canada through IPOs. Your savings rate and salary growth will do that, not IPO flipping (see file 07).
