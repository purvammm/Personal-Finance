# 4. Evaluating one IPO in 15 minutes (Prompt 4)

This is a repeatable checklist and a 0–20 scorecard. The same rules drive the IPO Radar ([file 05](05-ipo-radar-plan.md)). It's a decision aid, not advice. Data as of 2 October 2026.

## Where the documents are

- **SEBI → Filings → Public Issues.** This lists DRHPs (draft prospectuses), RHPs (the final ones, with price band and dates) and final prospectuses. For example, the RHPs for Runwal Enterprises and SRIT India appeared there on 23 Sep 2026.
- **NSE and BSE issue pages.** These carry the RHP, the abridged prospectus, bid details and the anchor allocation notice. Anchors are allotted the working day before the issue opens.
- **The lead managers' (BRLM) and registrar's websites.**
- **Fastest first read: the abridged prospectus.** It's a short summary, and SEBI standardised its format in 2026.

## What to read first in the RHP (in this order)

1. **Offer document summary** (first ~20 pages): business, issue structure, financials, litigation and related-party summaries, pre-IPO placements, and the **WACA** (weighted average cost at which promoters and investors acquired their shares).
2. **Objects of the Offer**: how much money goes to what. GCP (general corporate purposes) is capped at 25% on the mainboard.
3. **Basis for Offer Price**: key metrics (KPIs), listed peers' P/E, and WACA vs the offer price.
4. **Capital Structure**: promoter holding before and after the issue, lock-ins, **pledges**, recent allotments.
5. **Risk Factors**: the first ten.
6. **Restated financials and MD&A** (management's discussion and analysis): revenue, EBITDA, profit, **operating cash flow**, receivables.
7. **Outstanding Litigation**.
8. **Auditor's report**: qualifications, emphasis of matter, and any auditor changes.

## The 15-minute flow

| Minutes | Do this |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | **Basics:** board, size, fresh vs OFS, who is selling, Reg 6(1) or 6(2), category split, price band, lot, dates, registrar |
| 3–7 | **Numbers:** 3 years of revenue, EBITDA margin, PAT, operating cash flow, net worth and debt; P/E at the top of the band vs peers |
| 7–10 | **Red-flag scan** (table below) |
| 10–12 | **Anchors and use of proceeds** |
| 12–15 | **Closing day, after ~2 PM:** subscription by category and GMP trend → score → label → write your listing-day plan |

## Red flags: where to look

| Red flag | Where | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Promoter shares pledged | Capital structure, "encumbrance" | Lenders can force sales; often a sign of promoter stress |
| Related-party deals | Related-party note in the financials | Sales or costs may not be at market prices |
| Heavy OFS or promoter exit | Selling-shareholder table | The money goes to sellers, not the business. Ask why they're selling now |
| Early investors paid far less | WACA table | Holders who bought at, say, under half the offer price within 18 months are cashing out at a markup |
| Weak profit quality | Cash flow statement, working-capital days | Profit that isn't turning into cash. Nityas Gems reported a profit but **−₹14.7 crore** operating cash flow in FY26 |
| Circular revenues | Top customers/suppliers that are related or overlapping | Round-tripping inflates sales |
| Auditor change or qualification | Auditor section and report | A resignation or a going-concern note is serious |
| Litigation | Litigation section, compared with net worth | Large tax or regulatory claims; criminal cases against promoters |
| Concentration | Risk factors | One plant, top-5 customers above 50%, top-10 suppliers above 80% (Nityas: top-10 suppliers were 86% of purchases) |
| Profit jump just before the IPO | 3-year PAT | Possible window-dressing |
| Vague use of proceeds | Objects of the Offer | GCP at the cap, or "unidentified acquisitions" |

## Valuation, proceeds, anchors, subscription

- **P/E at the upper band** = (post-issue shares × upper price) ÷ latest full-year profit.
  - Compare it with **comparable** listed peers from "Basis for Offer Price". Check size, margins and business mix; companies often pick flattering peers.
  - Use EV/EBITDA (enterprise value ÷ operating profit) for indebted, capital-heavy firms, and price-to-book for lenders.
  - No listed peers means you should demand a margin of safety.
- **Use of proceeds.**
  - Good: capex with a timeline, debt repayment, or working capital backed by numbers.
  - Weak: large GCP, unnamed acquisitions, or paying promoters or related parties.
- **Anchors.**
  - Look for domestic mutual funds, insurers and pension funds; 40% of the anchor book is now reserved for them.
  - Be wary of books made up of small, unknown foreign funds.
  - Note the lock-in expiry dates (30 and 90 days). They bring future selling pressure.
- **Subscription.**
  - Days 1–2 are mostly retail noise. Institutions and big HNIs bid on the last afternoon, so decide on the **closing-day ~2 PM numbers**. Most brokers stop taking bids around 4–5 PM, and the mandate deadline is 5 PM.
  - The QIB figure only counts if the QIB quota is a meaningful share of the issue. Vishal Nirmiti reserves only ~1% for QIBs, while Nityas (a Reg 6(2) issue) must give ≥75% to QIBs.

## What GMP does and doesn't tell you

GMP is a noisy, unofficial quote from an unregulated market with no investor protection. A few dealers can move it, and it changes by the hour. **Direction is often right; size often isn't.**

| IPO | GMP shortly before listing | Actual opening vs issue price |
|---|---|---|
| A-One Steels | ₹47 ≈ +11.6% (InvestorGain, 1 Oct); other sites 8.6–14% | +12.35%, but it closed at +2.85% |
| Moneyview | about +38% (ET, 1 Oct) | +62% |
| Elevate Campuses | ₹6.5 ≈ +1.8% (InvestorGain, 30 Sep) | **−1.9%** |
| ArMee Infotech | ≈ +14% early in the issue (NDTV Profit, 23 Sep) | 0%, then −15% intraday |
| Vishal Nirmiti (still open) | ₹35 (16%) on 1 Oct → ₹20 (9%) on 2 Oct | — |

**GMP rules:**
- Trend matters more than level.
- Use at least two sites.
- It's worth at most 1 point on the scorecard.
- Never trade in the grey market, including "kostak" or "subject-to" deals.

## SME-specific risks (excluded by your rules for now)

- **Exit:** a market maker must quote for 3 years, but liquidity is still thin and you trade in big lots.
- **Ticket size:** the minimum application is **two lots, typically ₹2.4–2.9 lakh**.
- **Issuer rules:** OFS ≤20%; ≥₹1 crore EBITDA in 2 of the last 3 years.
- **Lock-ins:** promoters' excess holdings unlock in stages, and anchors after 30/90 days.
- **Price limits:** NSE caps the opening price at 90% above issue. 5% bands and trade-for-trade are common, so you can be stuck in a circuit in either direction.
- **Disclosure:** smaller auditors and a history of manipulation cases.
- **Possible change:** SEBI is discussing raising the QIB share of SME issues to up to 50% (Aug 2026, not final).
- **If you ever try SME:** use only money you could leave locked for a year, and only issues with real institutional demand.

## The scoring sheet

### Step 1 — Knock-outs (any one → **SKIP**)

| # | Knock-out |
|---|---|
| K1 | SME issue, while your risk limits say `sme_allowed: false` |
| K2 | One lot costs more than your per-IPO cap (₹15,000) |
| K3 | Auditor qualification, going-concern doubt, or an unexplained auditor resignation in the last 3 years |
| K4 | Promoter shares pledged, or SEBI/ED/criminal action against the company or its promoters |
| K5 | Negative net worth, or a loss in the latest year with no credible path to profit |
| K6 | At ~2 PM on the closing day: total below 1x, or QIB below 1x where the QIB quota is ≥30% of the issue |
| K7 | You can't explain in two sentences how the business makes money |

### Step 2 — Score (0–20). Unknown or unchecked = 0 points (not knowing is a risk)

| Factor | Points | Max |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Growth and consistency** | +1 if revenue grew ≥15% a year over 2–3 years · +1 if profit was positive every year with no drop over 50% · +1 if the EBITDA margin is stable or rising | 3 |
| **B. Cash and balance sheet** | +1 if operating cash flow was positive in the latest year · +1 if debt/equity is ≤1 after the issue · +1 if receivable/working-capital days are not ballooning | 3 |
| **C. Valuation** | P/E ÷ median comparable-peer P/E: ≤0.8 → 4 · ≤1.0 → 3 · ≤1.2 → 2 · ≤1.5 → 1 · higher (or no peers and P/E >40) → 0 | 4 |
| **D. Issue structure** | +1 if the fresh issue is ≥50% · +1 if all three hold: the objects are concrete with amounts, promoters sell ≤~20% of their stake, and WACA isn't far below the offer price | 2 |
| **E. Clean checks** | +1 if related-party transactions are immaterial · +1 if litigation is immaterial vs net worth · +1 if there's no heavy customer, supplier or single-plant concentration | 3 |
| **F. Anchors** | +1 if at least two domestic mutual funds or insurers are in the anchor book | 1 |
| **G. Closing-day demand** (~2 PM) | +1 if QIB (excluding anchors) is ≥10x (score 0 and flag it if the QIB quota is under 30%) · +1 if NII is ≥10x · +1 if retail is ≥3x | 3 |
| **H. GMP** | +1 only if it's ≥10% on two sites and hasn't fallen over two days | 1 |

### Step 3 — Label

| Result | Label | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Any knock-out | **SKIP** | Nothing |
| Score ≥14 **and** G ≥2 | **APPLY** | 1 lot, retail, cut-off; approve the mandate straight away |
| Score 10–13, or G <2 | **WATCH** | Paper-trade it: log the decision and the outcome |
| Score ≤9 | **SKIP** | — |

You may *downgrade* a label for a reason you write down. Never *upgrade* one ("but the GMP is huge").

### Step 4 — Listing-day plan (write it before listing day)

- **Opens above the issue price:** sell within the first 30 minutes after 10:00, or place a limit sell in the 9:00–9:45 pre-open.
- **Locked at the upper circuit** (5% band on issues of ₹250 crore or less): hold until it unlocks, then sell.
- **Opens below the issue price:** exit the same day. The only exception is a score ≥16 *plus* a 12-month thesis you wrote beforehand.
- **Never buy more on listing day.**

## Worked example: A-One Steels India (listed 1 October 2026)

| Item | Facts |
|---|---|
| Business | Integrated steel maker (sponge iron, billets, TMT bars, HR/CR coils, pipes, ferro-alloys). Six plants in Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh; capacity 17.33 lakh tonnes a year |
| Issue | ₹405 cr = **₹355 cr fresh** (87.65 lakh shares) + **₹50 cr OFS** by three promoters (₹20 + ₹20 + ₹10 cr). Band ₹385–405, lot 37 (₹14,985), open 24–28 Sep. Standard split: QIB ≤50%, retail ≥35% |
| Objects | ₹250 cr to prepay debt; the rest general corporate purposes |
| FY24 / FY25 / FY26 | Revenue ₹3,834 / 3,542 / 4,149 cr · EBITDA margin 4.5% / 4.9% / 7.3% · PAT ₹38.9 / **7.7** / 127.4 cr · net worth ₹422 / 677 / 820 cr · borrowings ₹1,043 / 964 / 1,011 cr · D/E 2.34 / 1.34 / 1.17 |
| Valuation | Post-issue market cap ≈ ₹3,128 cr at ₹405 (7.72 crore shares) → **P/E 24.6x FY26**. RHP peers: Shyam Metallics 28.3x, Jai Balaji 45.8x, MSP Steel 61.5x |
| Anchors | ₹120.9 cr on 23 Sep. Morgan Stanley was the largest (20.7% of the book), then Longthrive Capital (16.5%); Taurus MF was also named. Lock-ins end 2 Nov 2026 (50%) and 4 Jan 2027 |
| Final subscription | 12.2x total · QIB (excl. anchors) 7.7x · NII 25.8x · retail 9.1x |
| GMP | 8.6–14% across sites; ₹47 (≈11.6%) on 1 Oct, down from ~14% on 28 Sep |
| Listing | NSE open **₹455 (+12.35%)**, BSE ₹462 (+14.07%); NSE close **₹416.55 (+2.85%)** |

*Data-quality catch:* IPO Central's table shows a 5.24% FY26 net margin, but ₹127.41 ÷ ₹4,148.57 = **3.1%** (Chanakya Nipothi also reports ~3%). Recompute numbers yourself.

**Knock-outs:** none visible in public summaries. K3 (auditor) and K4 (pledges, actions against promoters) need the RHP, so check them.

| Factor | Strict score (verified only) | Why | If the RHP checks come back clean |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1 | Revenue grew only ~4%/yr (FY24→26); PAT fell 80% in FY25; margin rising (+1) | 1 |
| B | 1 | Debt/equity ≈0.65 after ₹250 cr repaid and ₹355 cr raised (+1); cash flow and working-capital days not verified | 3 |
| C | 3 | 24.6x ÷ 28.3x (Shyam Metallics, the closest comparable) = 0.87. Against the median of all three (45.8x) it would score 4, but those peers differ a lot | 3 |
| D | 1 | 88% fresh (+1); objects concrete and promoters sold little, but WACA not verified | 2 |
| E | 1 | Diversified customers: 1,246 retail channels, 32 distributors, 57 institutions (+1); related-party deals and litigation not verified | 3 |
| F | 0 | Only one domestic mutual fund confirmed (Taurus) | 0–1 |
| G | 2 | NII 25.8x (+1), retail 9.1x (+1), QIB 7.7x (0). These are *final* figures; at 2 PM they were lower | 2 |
| H | 0 | Sites disagreed (8.6–14%) and the trend was falling | 0 |
| **Total** | **9 → SKIP** | | **14–15 → APPLY** |

**What happened:**
- Retail allotment odds were **at least 11%** (94,079 lots ÷ ≤8.55 lakh applications).
- An allotted lot sold at the open made about **₹1,850** gross (≈₹1,430–1,815 after costs and tax).
- Held to the close, it made only ≈₹430.
- Expected value per application ≈ 11% × ₹1,850 ≈ **₹200**.

**Lessons:**
1. **The verdict hinges on 15 minutes in the RHP.** Five unchecked items decide it: cash flow, working capital, WACA, related-party deals, litigation. Summary websites can't replace that reading.
2. **Even a correct APPLY earns little.** With 1-lot retail odds, it's worth a couple of hundred rupees in expectation. The scorecard's main job is avoiding the −15% days, not finding jackpots.
3. **Pre-set exit rules matter.** "Sell in the first 30 minutes" captured most of the pop; waiting gave three-quarters of it back.
