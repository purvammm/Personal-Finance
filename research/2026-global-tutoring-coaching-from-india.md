# 2026 execution plan for earning globally from India through tutoring and coaching

**Execution memo for an India-based solo operator — evidence snapshot: September 16, 2026**

## Executive decision

Build a location-independent **AI Workflow Tutoring Sprint** for English-speaking independent consultants and small professional-service firms. Teach an adult client to turn one recurring, low-risk work process—research, meeting follow-up, proposal drafting, content repurposing, or internal documentation—into a repeatable AI-assisted workflow with human quality checks.

Do not launch as a generic “AI coach.” Sell a bounded result:

> In four weeks, I help an independent consultant build, test, and document one safe AI-assisted workflow they can operate themselves—without coding, hype, or handing over confidential client data.

Start with three paid beta clients at **$249 each**, move to **$499** after two documented outcomes, and add a small **$199-per-person cohort** only after the one-to-one curriculum works. At the planning conversion rate of ₹90/USD, those prices are approximately ₹22,410, ₹44,910, and ₹17,910. Actual INR receipts will differ with exchange rates, fees, and taxes.

This plan is location-independent, not jurisdiction-independent: it can be delivered from any place with reliable internet and lawful payment access, but the operator must follow the rules where they live. It is a business plan, not a promise of income. Survival pressure is a reason to protect cash, tell the truth, and collect evidence quickly—not to fabricate credentials, spam, borrow recklessly, or guarantee outcomes.

## What is verified and what is estimated

- **V-O — verified official:** a current policy, fee, eligibility rule, or product capability from an official source.
- **V-R — verified report:** research or marketplace observations that indicate direction but do not prove this operator’s demand.
- **E — estimate:** a planning assumption or scenario, not a market average or forecast.

The [World Economic Forum’s 2025 report](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/digest/) identifies AI/big data and technological literacy among the fastest-growing skill areas. **V-R.** Microsoft’s [2025 Work Trend Index](https://news.microsoft.com/annual-work-trend-index-2025/) describes widespread organizational redesign around AI, based on a global survey, product telemetry, and LinkedIn labor data. **V-R.** These sources support testing AI education; they do not prove that strangers will buy this offer.

The offer, prices, conversion funnel, revenue scenarios, and deadlines below are **E**. They must be validated with paid transactions.

## Non-negotiable qualification gate

Only teach what can be demonstrated honestly. Before selling the default offer, complete all five tests:

1. Build three sample workflows from blank inputs without copying a course: meeting notes to action plan; research to cited brief; and rough outline to client-ready proposal draft.
2. For each sample, document inputs, tool settings, prompt/process steps, human checks, likely failure modes, and what information must never be entered.
3. Record a 10-minute screen-share lesson explaining one workflow in clear English without reading a script.
4. Ask two working professionals to follow one guide. At least one must complete it without live rescue and rate the explanation 4/5 or better.
5. Correctly explain hallucination risk, confidential-data handling, verification, access controls, and why AI output needs accountable human review.

**Pass:** begin the 48-hour launch below. **Fail:** spend seven days improving the weak area, then retest. Do not claim to be an AI engineer, certified coach, teacher, or industry expert unless that is true and provable.

### If AI workflow tutoring is not a genuine strength

Use the same business system with the strongest subject that passes this scorecard. Score each item 0–5 and choose only a subject scoring at least 20/25.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Demonstrable competence | Can I solve real problems live and explain why? |
| Buyer value | Does success save time, make work easier, improve a job-relevant skill, or remove a costly mistake? |
| Proof | Can I show work samples, results, credentials, or references truthfully? |
| English delivery | Can I teach the subject clearly to an international adult learner? |
| Repeatable curriculum | Can I define a four-week before/after result? |

Good substitutes include Python debugging for working analysts, SQL for non-technical operators, business-English presentations, portfolio critique, or a specific exam only when the operator has real competence. Avoid generic motivation/life coaching, therapy-like claims, financial/legal/medical coaching, academic cheating, and subjects in which the only “expertise” is AI-generated answers.

## Exact customer and problem

### Initial ideal customer profile

Target one-person consultants and very small agencies that:

- sell expertise in English to businesses;
- already pay for at least one AI tool or actively experiment with one;
- repeatedly create proposals, briefs, meeting notes, research, or educational content;
- can approve a $249–$499 professional-development purchase without procurement;
- have a public professional profile and evidence of active client work;
- do not need the tutor to access regulated, secret, or personally sensitive data.

Do not target “everyone who wants to learn AI.” For the first 30 days, select one role after interviews: marketing consultants, operations consultants, fractional executives, independent designers, or career coaches. Never use a client’s private information to train examples.

### Jobs the client is buying

The client is not buying prompts. They are buying:

1. selection of a workflow worth improving;
2. an understandable process they can repeat;
3. fewer blank-page starts and fewer inconsistent outputs;
4. a quality-control method;
5. safe practice on synthetic or approved data;
6. accountability to finish.

### Scope and exclusions

**Included:** one workflow; four calls; three async reviews capped at 20 minutes each; editable templates; quality checklist; final standard operating procedure; 48-business-hour response time.

**Excluded:** custom software; API integration; account administration; handling passwords; entering client systems; processing confidential customer data; unlimited messaging; legal/compliance certification; guaranteed time savings, revenue, employment, grades, or business results.

If the requested workflow needs code, regulated data, system access, or production automation, narrow it to teaching and prototyping with synthetic data or refer it to a qualified practitioner.

Activate after at least two founding clients complete the program and at least one reports a verified useful result. Same core scope, with stronger templates and refined curriculum. Payment is $499 upfront or two payments of $275; the second is due before lesson three.

Only after five completions. Up to four people from one firm, two workflows at most, six live hours, and one team guide. Quote after a paid diagnostic. Do not offer custom implementation inside this price.

Only after five one-to-one clients and three written outcome stories. Six to ten adults, four weekly 75-minute classes, office hour, workbook, and peer practice. Minimum six paid seats before the cohort runs; otherwise refund or reschedule with consent.

## Four-week curriculum

### Before lesson 1 — intake and baseline

Collect the workflow, current time per run, frequency, examples using synthetic/redacted data, tool access, desired result, and success metric. The client signs scope, privacy, cancellation, and recording terms before the session.

### Week 1 — choose and map

- Map trigger, inputs, decisions, output, reviewer, and failure cost.
- Classify data: public, internal, confidential, personal, or regulated.
- Choose only public/synthetic/explicitly approved inputs for exercises.
- Define one baseline metric: minutes per run, revision count, or completion consistency.
- Homework: run the current process once and note friction.

### Week 2 — build the first assisted version

- Teach task decomposition and context design.
- Build the smallest reusable instruction/template.
- Add examples and a required output format.
- Create a verification checklist and escalation rule.
- Homework: run the new workflow twice and save outputs plus corrections.

### Week 3 — test and improve

- Compare output against the checklist, not against “looks good.”
- Identify recurring errors, weak inputs, and unnecessary steps.
- Add citations/source checks where research is involved.
- Create version 2 and a fallback manual path.
- Homework: run three repetitions and record time plus defects.

### Week 4 — hand over and sustain

- Final observed run by the client.
- Finalize SOP, templates, data rules, and quality checks.
- Compare baseline and end measurement without exaggerating causality.
- Build a 30-day practice plan.
- Ask for feedback, testimonial permission, and one referral only after value is delivered.

### Low-cost stack

- Calls: Google Meet; its official feature page says one-to-one meetings can run up to 24 hours on the free product, while larger free meetings have shorter limits ([source](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/13396001?hl=en-GB)). **V-O.**
- Scheduling: Google Calendar appointment schedule or Calendly free tier, subject to current plan limits.
- Documents: Google Drive with one folder per client and least-privilege sharing.
- Forms: Google Forms or Tally.
- CRM: one spreadsheet until 30 active leads.
- Recording: off by default. Record only with explicit written consent and a deletion date.
- Backup: phone hotspot, wired headset, alternate meeting link, and a lesson PDF.

Do not buy a course platform, CRM, premium website, ads, or automation subscriptions before three paid clients.

### Session checklist

**24 hours before:** confirm payment, agenda, homework, and approved sample data. Test links.

**During:** restate objective; teach one concept; let the client operate the tool; observe; correct; capture next action. The client should do at least half the screen-driving.

**Within 24 hours after:** send a five-bullet recap, template version, homework, due date, risks found, and next meeting. Log delivery and preparation time.

### Cancellation and refund policy template

- Reschedule without charge with at least 24 hours’ notice.
- One late reschedule is allowed per sprint for a genuine emergency.
- A no-show or repeated late cancellation uses the session.
- If the tutor cancels, the client chooses rescheduling or a pro-rata refund.
- Before lesson 1, refund minus non-refundable payment fees if lawful and clearly disclosed.
- After lesson 1, refund undelivered sessions only; delivered diagnostics, sessions, and custom materials remain chargeable.

Have the final terms reviewed for the governing law and payment channel. Platform policies override personal terms for platform contracts.

### Objection responses

**“I can learn this on YouTube.”**

> You probably can learn the concepts there. The paid value here is choosing one workflow, practicing on your case, receiving feedback, and leaving with a tested guide. If accountability and customization are not valuable, free material is the better choice.

**“Can you guarantee time or money saved?”**

> No. We can measure a baseline and test the workflow, but results depend on the task, tools, and continued use. I guarantee the listed teaching and deliverables, not a business outcome.

**“Can you build it for me?”**

> This offer teaches you to operate the workflow. I can prototype with synthetic data, but I do not take system access or accept production implementation under this scope.

**“Why should I trust you?”**

> You should verify the sample, scope, and references rather than trust a title. I can show [real sample/proof], explain failure cases live, and start with the $49 diagnostic.

All scenarios are **E**, not expected earnings. The base requires successful sales, delivery, proof, payment collection, and roughly 25–32 work hours per week including acquisition. The upside is not a 90-day target and should not be attempted until group delivery and boundaries are proven.

### Capacity rule

For the first six clients, cap weekly work at:

- 10 hours live delivery;
- 8 hours preparation/review;
- 10 hours sales/content;
- 4 hours admin/bookkeeping;
- 3 hours skill improvement;
- total: 35 hours.

No more than three live calls in a day, two late-evening days in succession, or six concurrent one-to-one clients. Raise price or add a cohort before adding unsustainable hours.

## Twelve-week execution plan

### Week 1 — interview, do not overbuild

**Actions:** 50 personalized first messages; five warm introduction asks; five interviews; one proof asset; three relevant marketplace proposals.

**Deliverables:** exact language of pain; selected role/workflow; revised landing page; 100-prospect list.

**Gate:** at least five replies and three calls from 50 suitable contacts. If lower, improve targeting and first line before increasing volume.

### Week 2 — collect the first payment

**Actions:** 50 new messages; 25 follow-ups; five calls; ask for $49 diagnostic or $249 sprint; publish second proof asset.

**Gate:** at least one paid diagnostic or sprint by day 14. If none after 10 qualified calls, review competence, proof, buyer urgency, scope, and trust. Change one variable, not all five.

### Weeks 3–4 — deliver beta and measure

**Actions:** onboard up to three founding clients; deliver curriculum; track baseline, time, defects, client attendance, prep time, and questions.

**Gate by day 30:** two paying clients and one completion or strong mid-program signal. If outreach created calls but no sales, revise offer/risk reversal. If sales created poor engagement, revise qualification and curriculum.

### Weeks 5–6 — turn results into proof

**Actions:** finish clients; collect structured feedback; create an anonymized case study only with permission; tighten templates; raise price to $499 after two good completions.

**Gate:** at least 70% of enrolled clients complete; average delivery time trends toward 6.5 hours; one client can run the process without tutor intervention. Otherwise keep beta pricing and fix delivery.

### Weeks 7–8 — make acquisition repeatable

**Actions:** choose the best-performing channel; request referrals; approach 10 complementary partners; publish one deep case study and one teaching demonstration; continue 50 targeted messages weekly.

**Gate:** one channel produces at least two qualified calls per week for two weeks. If not, narrow role/workflow further or test a stronger proof asset.

### Weeks 9–10 — test standard price

**Actions:** quote $499 consistently; close two standard clients; remove low-value support; document SOPs; maintain six-client cap.

**Gate:** at least one $499 sale without unplanned scope. If beta sold but standard does not after 10 qualified calls, strengthen proof, reduce scope uncertainty, or test $399—not permanent discounting.

### Weeks 11–12 — decide the next engine

If five clients are complete, three outcome stories exist, and delivery is stable, pre-sell a six-seat cohort at $199 per seat. Do not build cohort materials until the minimum seats pay.

**90-day continue gate:**

- $1,500+ collected in at least one month;
- five completed clients;
- two permissioned testimonials or case studies;
- at least 70% completion;
- direct effective delivery contribution at least $35/hour before sales/admin/tax;
- one channel reliably produces qualified calls;
- no unresolved privacy, refund, or compliance failures.

If fewer than four conditions are met, do not scale. Run a two-week diagnosis, choose a new narrow subject/buyer if evidence supports it, or pursue employment/contract work in parallel.

## Metrics dashboard

Track weekly:

| Funnel | Delivery | Money | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualified prospects added | Attendance/show rate | Cash collected, not merely invoiced | Refunds/chargebacks |
| First messages | Completion rate | Gross and realized INR | Sensitive-data incidents |
| Positive replies | Client-operated final runs | Provider/platform fees | Platform concentration |
| Calls booked/held | Baseline vs final metric | Contribution per client | Late-night hours |
| Paid diagnostics | Prep-to-live ratio | Effective hourly contribution | Scope exceptions |
| Sprint conversion | Referrals/testimonial consent | Tax reserve | Client concentration |

Definitions:

- **Positive reply rate** = interested replies / suitable first messages.
- **Held-call conversion** = paid clients / qualified calls held.
- **Effective hourly contribution** = receipts minus direct fees/refunds/variable costs, divided by delivery and preparation hours.
- **Acquisition hourly return** = contribution from won clients divided by all prospecting, content, and sales hours.
- **Platform concentration** = receipts from largest platform / total receipts.

Targets are decision aids, not universal benchmarks: positive reply ≥8%; 70% of booked calls held; 20% of qualified calls convert after beta proof; completion ≥70%; refunds <10%; no channel >60% of receipts by month six.

## Risk controls

| Risk | Early warning | Control |
|---|---|---|
| No real expertise | Cannot explain failures without AI help | Qualification gate; narrower subject; transparent proof |
| Commodity pricing | Prospects compare only hourly rates | Sell one bounded workflow and measured handover |
| Weak proof | Calls stall at trust | Demonstrations, paid diagnostic, permissioned case studies |
| Confidential data leak | Client sends raw customer/company files | Written prohibited-data rule; synthetic/redacted exercises; delete unneeded files |
| Hallucinated output | Client accepts polished errors | Source checks, test cases, review owner, fallback process |
| Scope creep | “One quick build” becomes implementation | Scope/exclusion list; change order; referral |
| Platform dependency | Most receipts/leads from one account | Direct/referral channel; export only data allowed by terms; cash buffer |
| Payment loss | Work begins before funds clear | Upfront payment or funded milestone; written scope; receipts |
| Misleading claims | Pressure to promise ROI | Promise delivery only; document estimates; no fake scarcity/testimonials |
| Burnout/time zones | Calls every late night | One region/month; call/day cap; two consecutive late-evening cap |
| AI tool changes | Lesson breaks after UI update | Teach principles; tool-neutral SOP; verify before session |
| Compliance error | Missing tax/remittance records | CA review; transaction folder; monthly reconciliation |
| Minor safeguarding | Child booking or parent request | Adults-only launch; decline until verified consent/safeguarding process exists |
| Desperation spending | Buying ads/courses before proof | ₹15,000/120-hour stop-loss; no debt-funded launch |

## Weekly founder review

Every Friday, answer in writing:

1. What cash was collected and what reached the bank?
2. Which action produced each qualified conversation?
3. Where did clients get stuck?
4. How many hours did sales and delivery actually consume?
5. What claim is still unsupported?
6. What should stop next week?
7. Is the tax reserve intact?
8. Is any client, platform, or channel more than 60% of receipts?
9. Did any work cross the teaching/implementation or privacy boundary?
10. What is the one experiment for next week?

## Cash-protection rules

1. Do not resign from reliable income based on leads, followers, or verbal promises. Use collected, repeatable revenue and personal runway to decide.
2. Do not borrow for ads, certifications, or software before paid validation.
3. Cap launch cash at ₹15,000: domain/simple page if needed, headset/backup connectivity, CA consultation, and small platform proposal costs. Use free tools otherwise.
4. Keep business receipts separate from the 25–30% planning tax reserve.
5. Ask for upfront payment on small packages and funded milestones on platforms.
6. Maintain a parallel immediate-cash lane: relevant freelance documentation/training jobs, warm-network paid diagnostics, and genuine subject tutoring. Do not wait for a “personal brand.”
7. If basic living needs are at immediate risk, prioritize stable contract/employment income and trusted local support alongside this experiment. A premium coaching pipeline is uncertain and takes time.

## Evidence gaps

- No representative source proves typical take-home earnings for a new India-based AI tutor. Marketplace profiles and platform marketing have severe selection bias.
- Public price listings do not reveal utilization, discounts, cancellations, unpaid preparation, customer-acquisition time, or tax.
- The target buyer’s willingness to pay $249–$499 is unverified until money is collected.
- Payment-provider eligibility, fees, FX spreads, supported methods, and documentation can change by account and transaction.
- GST export treatment depends on facts not established in this plan.
- AI education demand does not automatically create demand for this operator, niche, message, or curriculum.
- The currency conversion is illustrative and cannot be treated as stable income enhancement.

## Bottom line

Start with a truthful, adult-only, four-week AI Workflow Tutoring Sprint—not a course, agency, or vague coaching brand. Build three demonstrations, interview five buyers, send 50 personalized messages, and ask for a paid diagnostic or $249 founding sprint within 14 days. Use Upwork for platform-discovered work, Wise Business or another approved route for direct international invoices, and India-qualified accounting advice before treating receipts as exports.

The first objective is not “lots of money.” It is evidence: one payment, one completed result, one referral, and economics that survive all delivery and acquisition time. If that evidence appears, raise the price, standardize the curriculum, and add a cohort. If it does not, protect cash, change the niche based on interviews, and keep stable work in parallel.
