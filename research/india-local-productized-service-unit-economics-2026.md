# India local productized-service economics: a 90-day beginner model

**Decision memo for an India-based beginner — evidence and legal-status snapshot: 15 September 2026**

> **Not a promise, tax opinion, or legal opinion.** Every sales, conversion, refund, workload, and cash figure marked `E` is a planning assumption. The scenarios are deliberately bounded at 90 days and must not be presented as expected earnings. GST, income-tax classification, local licensing, contracts, privacy, and outreach rules can turn on facts not known here; the points requiring a CA or lawyer are called out explicitly.

## Recommendation in one page

Use a narrow, direct-to-business offer rather than “freelancing” generally:

**Trial Booking & Lead Recovery Sprint for independent gyms and fitness studios.** In seven calendar days, organize the client’s own opted-in enquiries in a simple CRM, define pipeline stages, install a consent-aware enquiry form and WhatsApp handoff, write approved response/follow-up templates, and train one staff member. Do not buy ads, promise rankings or bookings, scrape contacts, bulk-message members, collect health information, or act as the gym’s unregistered telemarketer.

| Commercial item | Planning price (`E`) | Scope boundary |
|---|---:|---|
| First two to four proof pilots | **₹6,000** each | Same narrow sprint, explicitly discounted for feedback/case-study permission; no custom integrations |
| Standard sprint from day 31 | **₹10,000** | One location, one pipeline, one form, up to five templates, one 60-minute handover, two revision rounds |
| Monthly care, optional | **₹4,000/month** | Weekly pipeline check, one report, template/process maintenance; client’s staff sends messages |
| Payment | **50% before kickoff, 50% before handover** | Work starts after cleared deposit; pass-through purchases require written approval |
| Capacity ceiling | **6 sprints + 5 care clients per 30 days** | About 87 delivery hours before sales/admin; do not exceed this as a beginner |

Why this offer: it is understandable to a local owner, uses mostly free tools, produces an auditable operational deliverable, can be sold without a marketplace, and avoids pretending that a beginner can guarantee leads. A generic “social-media package” is easier to compare on price and harder to tie to a controlled workflow.

The **base scenario** credits **₹98,000 of gross receipts in 90 days**, spends an estimated **₹15,690** on operating cash costs, ring-fences **₹19,600** across income-tax (cash plus any TDS credit) and service recovery/refunds, and leaves **₹62,710 of cash potentially available for owner draw** in the no-receivable convention described below. This is only one deterministic scenario. The conservative scenario leaves ₹7,150; the capacity-capped upside leaves ₹112,440. None includes the beginner’s living costs, laptop, or a quoted CA/lawyer fee.

## Evidence labels and important limits

- **`V-O` — verified official/primary:** government law/guidance or a vendor’s live price/policy page.
- **`V-R` — verified secondary report:** an identified professional analysis used because the official page is difficult to retrieve or interpret. It is not law.
- **`E` — estimate:** this memo’s price, funnel, hours, costs, reserves, or arithmetic.
- Live pages are dated by their stated update date where visible; otherwise **accessed 2026-09-15**.

- The model assumes a sole individual selling directly to Indian businesses, no employees, no paid advertising, no marketplace, no foreign receipts, and turnover far below the general GST threshold during these 90 days.

## 1. The productized service and delivery system

### Seven-day deliverable

1. **Kickoff and permissions:** signed scope; client identifies its authorized account owner; inventory only client-supplied enquiries and the consent source.
2. **Data minimization:** exclude medical history, diagnoses, injury notes, payment-card data, government IDs, and children’s data. Use name, contact channel, enquiry date, broad service interest, consent evidence, owner, stage, and next action only.
3. **Pipeline:** New enquiry → Permission/consent confirmed → Contacted → Trial proposed → Trial booked → Attended → Won/lost/opted out.
4. **Capture:** a short form with purpose-specific notice and an unchecked WhatsApp follow-up choice. Do not bundle unrelated promotions into service-contact consent.
5. **Templates:** acknowledgement, trial invitation, reschedule, one follow-up, and closure/opt-out. The client approves brand and factual claims.
6. **Operations:** free CRM or spreadsheet, named staff owner, response checklist, simple weekly counts, export/backup, and access removal at handover.
7. **Training and acceptance:** 60-minute recorded handover if the client agrees; two revision rounds; written acceptance checklist.

### Explicit exclusions

No advertising spend or media buying; no Google ranking promise; no revenue, lead, trial, or membership guarantee; no scraping; no purchased lists; no cold WhatsApp; no bulk SMS/voice; no medical or nutrition advice; no handling of member payments; no password sharing; no work on minors’ data; no custom API, chatbot, or WhatsApp Business Platform integration; no 24/7 support.

The outcome sold is **a functioning, documented lead-handling system**, not a numerical sales result. Client staff, offer quality, location, pricing, seasonality, existing consent quality, and response discipline remain outside the beginner’s control.

## 3. Acquisition model: assumptions, not industry benchmarks

Sell directly through owner referrals, nearby business networking, permission-based calls, tailored email to a published business address, and in-person visits at non-peak hours. A public phone number is **not** WhatsApp marketing consent. The freelancer’s own prospect list and the gym’s member/enquiry list are separate datasets and require separate controls.

### Funnel formulas

For each period:

```text
qualified conversations = targeted owner contacts × conversation rate
meetings/audits          = qualified conversations × meeting rate
raw wins                 = meetings/audits × close rate
modelled wins            = min(round(raw wins), delivery-capacity limit)
```

| Scenario | Contact → qualified conversation | Conversation → meeting | Meeting → paid | Uncapped contact → paid |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Conservative (`E`) | 10% | 25% | 20% | **0.50%** |
| Base (`E`) | 20% | 35% | 25% | **1.75%** |
| Upside (`E`) | 30% | 45% | 35% | **4.73%** |

“Contact” means one named, relevant owner/manager approached with one reason the workflow may matter; it is not one automated message. “Conversation” means the owner confirms a current follow-up problem and fit. A free audit without a confirmed problem is not a qualified conversation.

### Acquisition workload and capacity

The funnel also consumes time, so the model does **not** assume a flat six hours of selling per win. Activity-time assumptions (`E`) are:

```text
research + initial tailored contact = 0.10 hour per contact
qualified conversation              = 0.25 hour each
meeting/audit                        = 0.75 hour each
proposal/follow-up                   = 0.33 hour per meeting
batched local travel/networking      = 3–7 hours per period
```

For the base funnel this is about **31 hours** in days 8–30, **41 hours** in days 31–60, and **51 hours** in days 61–90—roughly 13–15 acquisition hours per new client, not six. The upside funnel consumes about 57, 70, and 78 acquisition hours in those periods before delivery. Conservative selling still consumes about 15, 22, and 28 hours even when wins are zero or one. These estimates include averaged research and batching; track real time. If contacts require travel or long audits, capacity falls quickly.

These rates should be replaced by the operator’s own data after every 50 contacts. Integer wins below are illustrative realizations around expected values; a real funnel is noisy, and zero wins remains possible in every period.

### Period-by-period operating assumptions

| Period | Conservative (`E`) | Base (`E`) | Capacity-capped upside (`E`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Days 1–7** | 10 owner contacts; no sale | 20 contacts; no sale | 30 contacts; no sale |
| **Days 8–30** | 80 contacts → 8 conversations → 2 meetings → **0 pilots** | 120 → 24 → 8 → **2 pilots** | 160 → 48 → 22 → raw 8; cap at **4 pilots** |
| **Days 31–60** | 120 → 12 → 3 → **1 standard sprint** | 160 → 32 → 11 → **3 sprints + 1 care client** | 200 → 60 → 27 → raw 9; cap at **6 sprints + 2 care** |
| **Days 61–90** | 150 → 15 → 4 → **1 sprint; 0 care realized** | 200 → 40 → 14 → **4 sprints + 3 care** | 220 → 66 → 30 → raw 10; cap at **6 sprints + 5 care** |

Care attach is an assumption, not recurring-revenue proof. The conservative plan uses a 25% planning rate but rounds the two-client 90-day cohort down to **zero** realized care clients; the base uses 40–60% as cohorts mature; upside uses 50%. Tiny cohorts make percentages unstable. Do not extrapolate lifetime value from three months.

## 4. Unit economics

### Per-unit formulas

```text
cash contribution = collected price
                    − payment cost
                    − client-specific cash cost
                    − service-recovery/refund allowance

economic contribution = cash contribution
                        − (delivery + acquisition/admin hours) × founder shadow rate

cash contribution margin = cash contribution ÷ collected price
```

Assumptions (`E`): founder shadow rate ₹250/hour; one standard sprint uses 12 delivery hours plus **14 acquisition/admin hours** at the base funnel’s approximate average; care uses 3 delivery plus 1 admin hour; ₹500 sprint-specific cash cost includes local meeting/acquisition and client delivery incidentals; care uses ₹100; 5% of receipts remains ring-fenced for rework/refund/service recovery. Acquisition cost varies by period—the 14-hour allocation is a unit-level planning average, while capacity must use the period workload above.

| Unit (`E`) | Pilot sprint | Standard sprint | Monthly care |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Collected price | ₹6,000 | ₹10,000 | ₹4,000 |
| Blended payment cost, 0.5% | (₹30) | (₹50) | (₹20) |
| Client-specific cash cost | (₹400) | (₹500) | (₹100) |
| 5% service-recovery/refund allowance | (₹300) | (₹500) | (₹200) |
| **Cash contribution** | **₹5,270** | **₹8,950** | **₹3,680** |
| Cash contribution margin | 87.8% | 89.5% | 92.0% |
| Founder time cost | (₹6,500) | (₹6,500) | (₹1,000) |
| **Economic contribution** | **(₹1,230)** | **₹2,450** | **₹2,680** |
| Economic contribution margin | (20.5%) | 24.5% | 67.0% |

The pilot deliberately loses money after valuing time; it buys proof and must be capped. If a standard sprint exceeds 12 delivery hours or acquisition averages above 14 hours for two consecutive wins, either narrow scope, raise price, improve the channel, or stop selling it.

### CAC and value discipline

- **Cash CAC (`E`)** is embedded in the ₹500 sprint-specific cost. Track actual travel, printing, event fees, phone charges, and referral fees by client.
- **Time CAC (`E`)** is about 14 hours × ₹250 = ₹3,500 per won sprint at the base funnel. It is higher in the conservative case and can exceed the unit’s economic contribution; track acquisition hours by period, not only by win. If it rises above ₹4,500 for two consecutive standard-price wins, tighten targeting/referrals or raise price before adding volume.
- **Care value in this memo** is only cash received within the 90-day window. No terminal value, annual contract, or “lifetime” multiple is claimed.
- Gross margin without valuing founder time can look excellent while the operator earns very little per hour. Always report both cash and economic contribution.

## 5. Ninety-day cash-flow scenarios

### Cash-flow rules

- Prices are assumed **without GST** because this beginner is assumed unregistered and below the applicable threshold. A registered supplier should normally quote `₹X + applicable GST`, collect tax separately, and never treat GST collected as revenue.
- “Gross receipts credited” below means invoice value for work that starts, completes, and settles in the displayed period. Each sprint contributes a 50% deposit and 50% final payment in that same period; care is paid in advance. The deterministic tables assume **no opening/closing work in progress, no receivables, and no late payment**. A timing and TDS sensitivity follows; real cashflow must track each installment.
- Fixed operating cash includes phone/data, local travel, a modest domain/printing/tool allowance, and rises with activity. Laptop and personal living cost are excluded.
- Total income-tax allocation is **15% of gross receipts**, made up of any client TDS credit plus the cash top-up needed to reach 15%. This is not a tax rate or liability calculation. Service-recovery/refund reserve is **5%**. Restricted cash and TDS credit are not available for owner draw.
- Gateway cost uses the 0.5% blended assumption. Variable delivery cost is ₹400 per pilot, ₹500 per standard sprint, and ₹100 per care client.
- A CA/lawyer quote is excluded because it is fact-specific. Keep a separate **₹10,000 professional-advice contingency (`E`)**; if spent, subtract it rupee-for-rupee from available cash.

## 6. Break-even, capacity, and stop-loss

### Break-even

Using a normal month with ₹3,500 fixed operating cash (`E`):

1. **Operating-cash break-even:** `₹3,500 ÷ ₹8,950 = 0.39`; therefore **one standard sprint** covers modeled business cash overhead, before personal draw and income-tax reserve.
2. **₹25,000 personal-draw break-even:** available contribution per standard sprint after 15% tax reserve is `₹10,000 − ₹50 − ₹500 − ₹500 − ₹1,500 = ₹7,450`. Required units are `(₹3,500 + ₹25,000) ÷ ₹7,450 = 3.83`; therefore **four standard sprints/month**.
3. **Mixed cash break-even:** three standard sprints and two care clients provide `3 × ₹7,450 + 2 × ₹3,080 = ₹28,510`, approximately covering ₹3,500 overhead plus a ₹25,000 draw.
4. **Fully loaded economic target:** with acquisition time valued, four standard sprints plus four care clients provide `4 × ₹2,450 + 4 × ₹2,680 = ₹20,520`; after ₹3,500 fixed cost, only **₹17,020 economic profit** remains. Even the stated ceiling of six sprints plus five care clients produces `6 × ₹2,450 + 5 × ₹2,680 − ₹3,500 = ₹24,600`, just below a ₹25,000 economic-profit target. The operator must improve acquisition efficiency, charge at least slightly more, add a care client without breaking capacity, or accept the lower result.

Do not call “one client covers costs” a viable livelihood. It only covers the small modeled operating bill.

### Stop-loss rules

| Gate | Stop, change, or continue rule (`E`) |
|---|---|
| **Before first client** | Spend no more than **₹2,500** on tools/branding and no more than **₹7,500 total commercial experiment cash** by day 30, excluding an independently chosen CA/lawyer consult. No ads. |
| **Day 14** | If 80 truly targeted contacts have produced fewer than 8 owner conversations, change niche list, message, or channel—not the website design. |
| **Day 30** | If there is no paid pilot after at least 120 targeted contacts and 8 qualified conversations, stop this offer for one week and diagnose buyer/problem/price. Do not “solve” it by buying automation. |
| **Day 60** | Stop or narrow if fewer than 2 total clients have paid, realized cash contribution is below ₹300 per founder hour, any material data incident occurs, or refund/recovery payouts exceed 20% of collections. |
| **Day 90** | Do not treat it as a full-time business if trailing-30-day collections are below ₹30,000, there are fewer than 3 qualified opportunities, or fewer than 2 clients have accepted the standard price. Keep it as side income or pivot. |
| **Always** | Pause sales at 6 active sprints/30 days or when deadlines, consent evidence, access removal, or two-revision scope cannot be honored. |

A stop-loss protects cash and reputation; it does not prove that the market is permanently bad.

## 7. Refund and acceptance boundary

Use a short written statement of work. A practical planning policy (`E`, lawyer review recommended) is:

- **Before kickoff/access is granted:** refund 100% of money actually received.
- **After kickoff but before the first configured draft:** refund 70% of money actually received. With a 50% deposit, this returns 35% of the total fee and retains 15% as the disclosed earned kickoff/discovery portion.
- **After first configured draft but before the final invoice/handover:** refund 50% of money actually received and transfer the usable draft after settlement. At this point only the 50% deposit should have been collected.
- **After the final 50% is cleared and checklist handover occurs:** no convenience refund for completed work; still correct a material scope non-conformance reported within seven days.
- **Monthly care:** cancel before the next billing date; no charge for the next cycle. No convenience refund for a completed month, but refund undelivered work.
- **Pass-through purchases:** non-refundable only when pre-approved, actually paid, documented, and transferred to the client where possible.
- The policy does **not** waive rights for deficient service, misrepresentation, unfair terms, fraud, or rights available under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 or other applicable law.

Never label the entire upfront amount “non-refundable” while leaving scope and acceptance subjective. Define deliverables, revision count, client dependencies, delay treatment, ownership/license, confidentiality, data return/deletion, liability allocation, and dispute venue. **A lawyer is needed** for a reusable client agreement, especially if serving consumers, franchises, regulated wellness/health businesses, or processing customer data at scale.

## 8. India tax, registration, and records

### GST: the beginner version, with caveats

1. **General service threshold:** CGST Act section 22 generally uses **₹20 lakh aggregate turnover** for registration and **₹10 lakh** in specified special-category states. For service suppliers, the commonly relevant ₹10 lakh states are Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, and Tripura (`V-O`; verify current state/facts with a CA).
2. **Aggregate means PAN-wide, all-India turnover**, not profit and not this one offer. It generally includes taxable supplies, exempt supplies, exports, and inter-State supplies of persons under the same PAN, while excluding GST and specified inward supplies.
3. **Inter-State services:** IGST Notification 10/2017 exempts persons making inter-State taxable **services** from registration while aggregate turnover stays within the section 22 threshold. Therefore “one client in another state always forces GST registration” is generally outdated for small service suppliers—but place-of-supply and exceptions matter.
4. **E-commerce caveat:** section 24 historically creates compulsory-registration cases for supplies through an e-commerce operator required to collect TCS. Central Tax Notification 65/2017 gives a threshold exemption to certain service suppliers through such operators. Section 9(5) notified services, operator roles, TCS status, state rules, and later amendments can change the answer. This memo assumes **no marketplace/operator**.
5. **Other compulsory cases:** section 24 and special provisions can override the normal threshold. Voluntary registration also creates filing/invoicing obligations even below threshold until properly cancelled.
6. **Rate/classification:** many marketing, IT, and business-support services are commonly taxed at 18%, but the correct SAC, nature of supply, place of supply, exemptions, and reverse-charge position are fact-specific. Do not print “18% GST” or collect it before confirming registration and classification.
7. **If registered:** issue GST-compliant invoices, separate CGST/SGST or IGST correctly, claim input tax credit only when valid, file returns, and retain GST records for the statutory period (commonly 72 months from the due date of the relevant annual return, longer if proceedings apply).

**CA required:** before crossing roughly ₹15–18 lakh of trailing aggregate turnover; before any voluntary GST registration; first out-of-state/foreign/marketplace engagement if the contract is unusual; if a client insists on GST; and for place-of-supply, SAC, reverse charge, input credit, or composition questions.

### Income tax and bookkeeping

- Business/professional profit is not the same as collections. Keep a cash/bank book, invoices/credit notes, signed scopes, bank and gateway statements, expense bills, asset records, TDS certificates, AIS/26AS reconciliation, client advances, refund records, and tax filings.
- Keep a separate business bank account or at least a dedicated account/ledger; reconcile monthly. Never spend GST collected or the model’s tax reserve.
- Income-tax Act section 44AA and Rule 6F determine required books for specified professions and threshold cases. “Technical consultancy” is listed, but whether this exact mixed creative/operations service is a profession or business is fact-dependent.
- Sections 44AD and 44ADA are not interchangeable. Section 44ADA is for specified professions; section 44AD is for eligible businesses and has exclusions. Digital-receipt thresholds and deemed-profit percentages do not eliminate the need for evidence of turnover, TDS, assets, or other income. **A CA must classify the activity before presumptive-tax treatment is chosen.**
- As a conservative operating rule, retain tax and contract records for **at least six years after the end of the relevant assessment year**, and longer where a return is late, an assessment/proceeding is open, an asset remains held, or another law/contract requires it.
- Advance tax can apply when estimated tax payable after TDS is at least ₹10,000; dates and presumptive-tax timing differ. Client TDS under section 194C or 194J may reduce cash received but should appear as credit in Form 26AS/AIS. Classification requires a CA.
- The model’s 15% reserve is only liquidity discipline. The live Income Tax portal for AY 2026–27 shows ITR-3 for business/profession and optional ITR-4 for eligible presumptive cases, but the correct return and actual tax depend on all income, deductions, regime, residency, and entity type (`V-O`).

### Udyam and entity/local registrations

- The official Udyam portal says registration is **free and paperless**. As of 15 September 2026 it shows the post-1 April 2025 **micro enterprise** limits as investment up to ₹2.5 crore and turnover up to ₹10 crore (`V-O`).
- Udyam is useful MSME recognition; it is **not** incorporation, GST registration, a trade licence, professional qualification, or proof that outreach/data practices are lawful.
- A sole proprietor can usually begin without forming a company, but state Shops and Establishments law, municipal trade licence, professional tax, home-office/lease terms, and local signage rules vary. Ask a local CA/company secretary or lawyer for the city/state checklist.
- Do not pay an unofficial site for Udyam; use only the government portal.

## 9. WhatsApp, TRAI, privacy, and data handling

### WhatsApp consent is a hard boundary

WhatsApp’s live Business Messaging Policy says a business may contact a person only when **both** (a) the person gave the mobile number and (b) the business received opt-in permission for subsequent WhatsApp messages or calls. It also requires honoring opt-outs; the Platform requires approved templates for business-initiated conversations and permits free-form replies only within the applicable 24-hour customer-service window (`V-O`).

Practical rule:

```text
No cold WhatsApp.
Public number ≠ opt-in.
Service updates ≠ permission for promotions.
Record source + timestamp + purpose + wording of opt-in.
Make STOP/opt-out simple and suppress immediately.
```

The freelancer should not send messages from a personal account on the gym’s behalf. The gym owns its account, approves templates, and sends messages; the freelancer configures process and trains staff. If the freelancer later operates the account or API, obtain a data-processing and messaging-responsibility agreement and lawyer review.

### TRAI commercial communications

TRAI’s current UCC hub identifies the **Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations, 2018 (TCCCPR)** framework and provides sender/telemarketer guidance for legitimate commercial communication (`V-O`). For commercial **SMS and voice** at scale, principal-entity/telemarketer registration, distributed-ledger registration, headers, content templates, consent templates/acquisition, customer preferences, and complaint consequences can apply. Do not use ordinary ten-digit numbers, robocalls, bulk SMS software, or unregistered vendors to bypass the framework.

WhatsApp is not a substitute DLT registration and DLT registration is not WhatsApp opt-in. TRAI’s telecom SMS/voice framework, platform rules, DPDP/privacy law, and consumer law are separate layers. The exact treatment of one-to-one prospect calls, click-to-WhatsApp campaigns, service versus promotional content, and a freelancer acting for a principal entity needs **telecom/privacy counsel** before scale. The safe launch path is referrals, in-person permission, tailored business email, and WhatsApp only after recorded opt-in.

### DPDP Act and Rules status on 15 September 2026

Government notification **G.S.R. 843(E), 13 November 2025** phases the **Act itself** (`V-O`):

- **In force from 13 November 2025:** section 1(2), section 2, sections 18–26, sections 35, 38–43, and sections 44(1) and 44(3). These principally enable definitions, the Data Protection Board/institutional machinery, government powers, rulemaking, and consequential provisions.
- **From 13 November 2026—not yet in force on this memo’s date:** section 6(9) and section 27(1)(d), associated with Consent Manager obligations/Board functions.
- **From 13 May 2027—not yet in force:** sections 3–5; sections 6(1)–(8) and 6(10); sections 7–17; section 27 except 27(1)(d); sections 28–34; sections 36–37; and section 44(2). These include the main processing grounds, notices/consent, fiduciary duties, rights, enforcement, penalties, information calls/blocking, and the remaining consequential amendment.

The **final Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025** were also notified on 13 November 2025 and follow a matching phased structure (`V-O` notification; `V-R` EY explanation):

- Rules **1, 2 and 17–21** took effect on Gazette publication.
- Rule **4** starts one year after publication, **13 November 2026**.
- Rules **3, 5–16, 22 and 23** start 18 months after publication, **13 May 2027**.

Therefore, on 15 September 2026 the Board/machinery and enabling provisions listed above are in force, while the main operational data-fiduciary duties are not yet commenced under the DPDP Act/Rules. That does **not** create a permission to mishandle data: other law, contracts, platform policy, confidentiality duties, and security expectations can apply already, and implementation work takes time. Counsel should verify any later amending notification and the operator’s role before launch or scale.

Minimum launch controls:

- map each field, purpose, source, user, vendor, and deletion date;
- collect the minimum; avoid health and child data entirely;
- give a clear purpose notice and capture affirmative, granular consent where relied upon;
- provide a contact for access/correction/deletion/opt-out requests;
- use unique accounts, MFA, least privilege, exports/backups, and an access-removal checklist;
- prohibit use of client leads to train AI or build the freelancer’s prospect list;
- document processors/vendors and prohibit unauthorized sub-processing;
- maintain an incident plan and tell the client immediately about loss or unauthorized access;
- return/export and delete client data at the contract end, subject to documented legal retention.

The gym is likely to determine purposes and means for member/enquiry data; the freelancer may act as its processor for that work while being an independent fiduciary/controller for the freelancer’s own prospect and billing data. Labels depend on facts. **Privacy lawyer required** before API automation, sensitive/health data, children, cross-border vendors, large-scale messaging, biometrics, or drafting a processor agreement.

## Bottom line

This can be a sensible **capped 90-day experiment**, not a guaranteed career. The cash risk is low only if the beginner uses free tools, takes deposits, refuses custom scope, avoids cold WhatsApp/bulk telecom messaging, protects client data, and obeys the stop-loss. The base case credits ₹98,000 of gross receipts, but only ₹62,710 is potentially drawable after modeled operating cash and reserves—and even that assumes no closing receivable and excludes living costs and professional fees. After valuing the funnel’s acquisition time, the economic result is materially weaker than cash margin suggests. Evidence of viability is not a polished website; it is repeated standard-price sales, controlled delivery and acquisition hours, low rework/refunds, documented consent, and a trailing pipeline that survives beyond friendly pilot clients.
