# Version 3 — India creative-income execution plan

**Evidence snapshot:** 15 September 2026

**Audience:** one India-based beginner with a recent iPhone, laptop, internet, modest capital, a creative inclination, and no assumed audience, sales history, specialist credential, or marketplace reputation

**Decision horizon:** 90 days

**One prescribed path:** a direct, local **Completed-Project Proof Sprint** for independent interior-renovation contractors

**Not reused from Versions 1 or 2:** this memo was built from a new twelve-route market scan, niche/workflow analysis, unit-economics model, legal/operational review, and adversarial red-team review.

> **The honest answer about guarantees:** no lawful business plan can guarantee a buyer, a sale, or a specific income. This plan guarantees only a concrete sequence of actions, spending limits, proof requirements, and stop rules. Revenue can be **₹0** even with correct execution. Anyone promising “1000% guaranteed” earnings is concealing demand, skill, timing, and luck risk.

## Executive decision

For the next 90 days, do not build SaaS, buy inventory, bid on Upwork, become a generic social-media manager, wait for creator monetisation, or learn broad “AI automation.” Sell one narrow outcome to one narrow local buyer:

> **Turn one completed renovation into truthful, client-owned proof assets and a measurable contact path in three business days.**

The first three eligible clients pay **₹4,900 plus applicable taxes** for one founder sprint. After a completed sprint, offer the exact same capped production system once per month for **₹12,000 plus applicable taxes**. There are no ads, cold lists, daily posting, inbox management, ranking promises, lead promises, or revenue guarantees.

### Why this path was selected

1. **It can be sold before an audience exists.** Prospects are identifiable from public business listings and local trade networks. Distribution is direct rather than algorithmic.
2. **The equipment is already owned.** The iPhone is useful for on-site capture; the laptop handles editing, delivery, invoicing, and measurement. The 90-day software budget is ₹0.
3. **It combines creativity with a business workflow.** The deliverable is not “make content.” It is project selection, permission, visual proof capture, consistent editing, a client-owned Google Business Profile update, a source-coded contact link, and a minimal enquiry log.
4. **One high-contribution renovation can make a ₹12,000 operating expense rational.** This is only a qualification test, not an ROI promise. The owner must estimate contribution; contract value is not contribution.
5. **The hypothesis can be disproved quickly.** Two cleared 50% booking payments of ₹2,450 each for engagements priced ₹4,900 by Day 14, sustainable delivery by Day 30, recurring demand by Day 60, and renewal plus attributable enquiry evidence by Day 90 decide whether it survives.
6. **It avoids the largest beginner risks in competing routes.** There is no inventory, ad spend, health data, speculative product build, audience dependency, or marketplace ranking dependency.

## What the buyer actually needs—and when they do not need you

Google Business Profile, WhatsApp, phone cameras, and editing apps are already available. The pitch must never be “you need these tools.” A qualified contractor pays for a missing operating capability:

- selecting a completed job that proves a commercially useful service;
- obtaining permission before handover or occupation;
- capturing a coherent wide/detail/process sequence in difficult interior light;
- producing reality-faithful assets in the formats the owner can reuse;
- publishing one accurate update without taking account ownership;
- installing a source-coded enquiry path and a minimal follow-up log;
- shipping consistently while the owner remains focused on site work and sales.

**Substitution test:** if a contractor already captures, edits, publishes, and tracks credible project proof every month, tell them they do not need this service. Selling to an already-solved buyer creates churn and reputational damage.

The buyer is not paying for an iPhone. The buyer is paying for selection, permission, reliable execution, truthful presentation, turnaround, and operational follow-through. Public evidence confirms that Google supports business photos, videos, updates, and performance data; it does **not** prove that a contractor will pay this operator or that an update will cause an enquiry. Only cleared deposits and renewals prove willingness to pay.

### 1. Local productized proof service

**Demand context:** India has an enormous micro-enterprise base on the official Udyam/UAP systems, and paid local home-service activity is visible in Urban Company’s public investor materials. Google provides free business profiles with photos, videos, updates, calls/clicks, and performance data. Meta reports, citing Kantar 2025, that 91% of online Indian adults chat with businesses weekly. These are adjacent demand and infrastructure signals—not proof that renovation contractors purchase a proof sprint.

**Why it wins for this profile:** no audience, stock, platform ranking, specialist credential, or codebase is required. The buyer list and visible proof gap can be inspected. Creative production is tied to a fixed operating deliverable. The business can collect a booking payment before delivery.

**Failure modes:** owners already solve the problem; no permissioned handover-ready sites; price sensitivity; weak interiors photography; long travel; scope creep; assets published but no qualified enquiries; one-off buying with no renewal.

### 2. Tutoring and coaching

MoSPI’s 2025 education survey reported widespread private coaching participation, making the underlying paid category better evidenced than most creative services. Cash can arrive quickly and margins can be high. It loses for this user profile because no demonstrable subject, examination result, language, or practical skill was provided. Teaching an unmastered skill is neither fast nor ethical.

### 3. Skilled offline service

Repair, cleaning, beauty, installation, and similar work can produce same-week cash through local referrals. This route ranks highly only when the operator already has safe hands-on competence, tools, and transport. Learning electrical, plumbing, beauty, repair, or installation work while serving paying customers creates safety and liability risk.

### 4. Events and weddings

Current Indian providers visibly sell rapid event-reel packages, proving that the product category exists. Deposits can arrive before event day. The beginner barrier is material: low light, battery/storage failure, missed rituals, overnight travel, music rights, guest privacy, conflict with the principal photographer, and severe date clustering. Retail package prices also do not reveal creator payout.

### 5. Employment and direct contracting

This may produce the highest income after an offer and carries little capital risk. NCS offers a free official jobseeker channel. It ranks lower for first cash because vacancy, shortlist, interviews, employer timing, and payroll are not controlled by the applicant. The plan therefore does not pretend that entrepreneurship is safer than employment; it simply selects the route that best fits the requested creative/direct-control constraint.

### 6. No-code or AI workflow implementation

A specific workflow—quotation follow-up, appointment reminders, invoice chasing, or enquiry routing—can become valuable. A generic “AI automation agency” is not a buyer outcome. A beginner must understand process mapping, permissions, failures, exception handling, support, and data protection. This becomes rational only after manual client work exposes one repeated, paid workflow.

### 7. Recruitment and referral placement

Low cash capital and direct employer outreach are attractive. Payment is often contingent on joining and retention, which can push collection outside 90 days. Candidate consent, discrimination, fake vacancies, replacement clauses, and personal-data handling create risk. Never charge jobseekers for supposed access to a vacancy.

### 8. E-commerce and reselling

ONDC and large marketplaces establish extensive Indian commerce activity. They do not establish beginner profitability. Stock, shipping, marketplace charges, GST/e-commerce conditions, ads, packaging, damage, returns, and price competition can make cash receipts look like profit while capital shrinks. Modest survival capital should not be placed into unvalidated inventory.

### 9. Creator and UGC

BCG reported a large Indian creator ecosystem and substantial creator-influenced spending; influenced spending is not creator income. Production-only UGC can pay without an audience, but buyers still require a portfolio, brand fit, rights negotiation, and outreach. Low-end barter and unclear usage rights create price pressure. In this plan, short-form production is a deliverable inside a direct local service, not the business model by itself.

### 10. Digital products

Templates and guides have low fulfilment cost but do not solve discovery. Marketplaces can charge meaningful transaction/discovery fees; a new seller still needs traffic, trust, support, and a specific urgent problem. Build a product only after repeated service delivery reveals recurring demand.

### 11. Local media

A neighbourhood page or newsletter can eventually become owned distribution. Before sponsorship, it needs audience habit and trust, making publishing a second job. Public representative 90-day revenue data for new Indian local publications is absent.

### 12. Micro-SaaS

SaaS can repeat after product-market fit but performs poorly on a first-cash horizon. Discovery, building, security, billing, support, and distribution all precede reliable revenue. Global solo-founder distributions are highly unequal and cannot be treated as Indian beginner base rates.

4. Site is within the travel boundary.

## Skill path: learn only what the offer requires

### Skill 1 — ethical discovery and sales

Learn to qualify a buyer, map the current process, ask for evidence, state exclusions, and request payment without exaggerating. Use the free HubSpot Academy Inbound Sales modules on identify/connect/explore/advise. Output: a qualification sheet, 12-minute discovery script, and ten recorded role-plays.

### Skill 2 — interior capture on iPhone

Use the rear 1× lens, clean lens, grid/level, locked exposure/focus, 4K or 1080p at 30 fps, slow intentional movement, daylight where possible, and a fixed shot list. Learn wide establishing views, medium functional views, detail/craft views, and truthful before/after only when provenance is verified. Output: one fictional/home sample with 12 photos and three silent clips.

### Skill 3 — editing and delivery

Use one editor already available to the operator; do not buy a course or subscription. Correct exposure, white balance, crop, vertical framing, cuts, and captions without adding/removing material features. Export a named folder with web-ready files and an approval sheet. Output: complete sample delivered within three business days and no more than 11 total production hours.

### Skill 4 — Google Business Profile operations

Understand ownership/manager access, media requirements, update policies, performance metrics, review rules, and third-party disclosure. Never request an OTP or retain ownership. Output: account-access checklist, client-side upload option, baseline screenshot template, and access-removal checklist.

### Skill 5 — measurement without false causality

Create a source code, minimal client-owned enquiry log, and definitions. A qualified enquiry is a new non-vendor/non-existing-client prospect in the service area who states a renovation need and enough budget/timeline information for fit. It is attributable only when the source code remains or the prospect explicitly identifies the project/update. Views, clicks, unknown-source calls, and “Hi” messages are not qualified attributable enquiries.

### Skill 6 — permissions, safety, and records

Use property/occupier permission, identifiable-person releases, factual claim approval, authorship/credit confirmation, access log, deletion schedule, invoice, and expense ledger. Strip location metadata from delivered media. Never film minors, sensitive documents, addresses, keys, alarms, valuables, or detailed floor plans.

- Maximum experiment cash through Day 30: **₹3,000** for local travel, data, and up to 20 one-page printouts.
- Software, domain, ads, courses, gear, lead lists, automation, and subscriptions: **₹0** before three fully paid sprints.
- Never invest rent, food, debt-payment, medical, or emergency money.
- Keep every receipt and invoice; ring-fence 20% of receipts until a CA calculates actual obligations.
- Use Zoho Invoice or another compliant record process; do not call an invoice “GST invoice” or collect GST unless entitled/required.

## Sales system

### Prospect source

Build the list manually from Google Maps, public websites, local interior-material stores, painters/carpenters who work under renovation contractors, architects who do not offer execution, property managers, and referrals. Initial acquisition is **primarily respectful in-person visits and warm introductions** at appropriate business hours. Do not make unsolicited calls or send unsolicited WhatsApp/SMS messages. A phone call or message is allowed only after that owner explicitly invites it or schedules it; record that permission and honour its scope. Do not scrape private data, blast messages, auto-dial, or buy lists.

### 20-second in-person/warm-introduction opener

> “Hi, I’m [name], based nearby. I turn one completed renovation into a truthful project-proof set—12 photos, three short videos, one Google update, and a trackable enquiry path. I’m not selling ads or promising leads. Before I show anything, may I ask three questions to see if you already solve this in-house?”

### Three qualification questions

1. “How many residential interior projects did you complete in the last 12 months, and is one handover-ready in the next 14 days?”
2. “Show me the last completed project you captured, published, and tied to an enquiry source.”
3. “Who owns approvals and enquiry follow-up, and can they update a simple status log weekly?”

If any core criterion fails, exit politely. Do not force a demo.

### Discovery questions

1. Walk me through the last project from handover to published proof.
2. What stops the team from capturing a complete set every time?
3. Which project type do you most want more of?
4. Which channels currently produce genuine enquiries?
5. Roughly how many genuine enquiries arrived in the last 90 days?
6. Who responds, and within what time?
7. What is gross contribution—not contract value—on a suitable won project?
8. Who owns the property and worker permissions?
9. Who must approve facts, brands, credits, and final media?
10. If this sprint produces excellent assets but no enquiry, is the fixed production output still worth ₹4,900?

### Close

> “The founder sprint is ₹4,900 plus applicable taxes: one permissioned completed site, 12 photos, three short videos, one approved Google update, one source-coded WhatsApp path, and a minimal client-owned log. Delivery is three business days after the shoot and complete approvals. It does not include ads, ranking, leads, daily posting, raw footage, reshoots, or unlimited revisions. A cleared 50% booking payment of ₹2,450 books the ₹4,900 engagement; the remaining ₹2,450 clears before final delivery. Would you like the written scope and payment details for [specific date]?”

### Objection handling

- **“We can shoot this ourselves.”** “You should if the process already ships consistently. If you can show one coherent recent set, an approved update, and source tracking, you do not need me.”
- **“Guarantee leads.”** “I cannot honestly guarantee demand or Google ranking. I guarantee only the written assets, turnaround, access rules, and measurement setup.”
- **“Do a free sample.”** “I have one generic sample. Custom site work starts only after scope, permission, and booking payment.”
- **“Pay after a sale.”** “No. I sell fixed production, not commission lead generation. Outcome-contingent payment would misprice factors I do not control.”
- **“Manage everything.”** “Not in this sprint. Daily posting, inbox handling, ads, and SEO are excluded.”
- **“Give us raw footage and unlimited changes.”** “The fixed price includes final listed exports and one consolidated edit revision. Additional rights or work need a separate written quote.”

## Delivery SOP

1. **Qualify:** all eleven criteria pass; owner contribution, demand, capacity, and proof gap are recorded.
2. **Scope and payment:** signed scope, booking payment, date, site, output, rights, exclusions, dependency clock, and cancellation terms.
3. **Permissions:** property/occupier, identifiable people, contractor authorship/credit, approved factual claims, and safe access.
4. **Pre-production:** choose one service story; create 12-photo and three-video shot list; identify prohibited details.
5. **Baseline:** capture current GBP media/update/performance state and existing enquiry process without copying unnecessary personal data.
6. **Shoot:** clean lens; grid/level; wide/medium/detail sequence; no minors, private documents, addresses, keys, alarms, plates, or unsafe positions.
7. **Back up:** encrypted local/client-approved storage; remove location metadata; name files consistently.
8. **Edit:** reality-faithful correction only; licensed/silent audio; no fabricated features or misleading before/after.
9. **Approval:** watermarked proof sheet; one consolidated revision; client confirms copy, claims, brands, credits, and permissions.
10. **Measurement:** create source-coded link and minimal client-owned log; define qualified/attributable enquiry.
11. **Final payment and delivery:** cleared balance, final exports, client-side upload or manager access, access log updated.
12. **Close:** publication status recorded separately from delivery SLA; remove unnecessary access; schedule 30-day review.

## 90-day execution architecture

The interactive HTML contains one required action and evidence output for every day. Its 90 entries remain distinct. The feasible phase logic is:

### Days 1–7 — build the complete field kit and pass readiness

Write the offer and exact 11-condition qualification sheet; complete the practical starter templates; produce one truthful, permissioned home/sample set of 12 photos and three clips; build the GBP baseline, source-code and enquiry-log demonstration; rehearse the opener, discovery and close; research all 40 prospects; and pass a closed-book readiness check. Learning is capped at 45 minutes/day and must produce an artifact. No owner outreach pitch occurs until the kit and readiness output exist.

### Days 8–14 — primarily in-person/warm outreach and paid-demand gate

Use primarily respectful in-person visits and warm introductions. Do not make unsolicited calls or send unsolicited WhatsApp/SMS; phone/message only after an owner explicitly invites or schedules it. By the end of Day 13, complete at least 40 owner attempts and 10 decision-maker conversations, qualify before demonstrating, present the fixed scope, and ask for payment. Day 14 requires two **cleared 50% booking payments of ₹2,450 each for engagements priced ₹4,900**. Compliments, barter, “later,” scopes without cleared funds, and free trials count as zero.

### Days 15–30 — deliver three founder sprints and test recurrence

Days 15–29 must scope, obtain permissions, shoot, edit, approve, fully collect, and deliver **three** paid founder sprints. Use the starter templates before every capture; gather delivery hours, direct cost, publication and outcome evidence; and ask each completed client for the unchanged ₹12,000 monthly package. Day 30 assesses three completed paid sprints, operating metrics, publication, and one cleared monthly payment.

### Days 31–60 — repeatable full-price monthly delivery

Deliver without adding scope, keep primarily in-person/warm selling before editing, collect baseline/performance/enquiry evidence, deliver enough unique clients to establish the first-five denominator, and reach three active full-price clients. By Day 60, median fulfilment must be ≤10 hours/client, direct cost ≤15%, and at least two of the first five delivered clients must record one qualified attributable enquiry within 30 days.

### Days 61–90 — renewal and viability proof

Standardise templates, QA, access removal, invoicing, permissions, and reporting. Reach five active full-price clients and test observed renewal. Day 90 viability requires ₹60,000 collected monthly revenue, three renewals among at least four eligible decisions, three of the first five clients with one qualified attributable enquiry, ≤55 monthly founder hours for five clients, and ≥₹900 contribution per founder hour after direct cost.

## Mandatory gates

Gate state is never inferred from elapsed time. Each criterion needs an explicit evidence check and short evidence note in the dashboard. A gate is **PASS** only when every listed criterion is checked; an explicit failure is **FAIL**; otherwise it is **UNASSESSED**. Reset removes the assessment rather than implying review. A serious permission, ownership, truthfulness, safety, privacy, or account incident is an immediate stop regardless of any gate status.

## Measurement definitions

### Founder sales funnel

`researched prospects → decision-maker conversations → qualified discoveries → written scopes → cleared booking payments → completed sprints → monthly buyers → renewal decisions → renewals`

Track counts and conversion only with real denominators. A proposal sent without a qualified discovery is not progress.

### Client operational funnel

`source-coded/confirmed enquiry → qualified → site call/consultation → quotation → won/lost`

The sprint does not claim causality for a won project. Report profile views/clicks, confirmed-source enquiries, and owner-updated outcomes separately.

### Cash ledger

Track gross receipt date, client, invoice, service period, payment method, direct travel/tool/rework cost, restricted 20% reserve, refund/service-recovery amount, TDS credit, and cash available. Revenue is not profit. TDS is a credit/receivable, not a fee, but it affects cash timing.

## India compliance and operating boundaries

This is operational guidance, not legal or tax advice.

### Outreach

TRAI actively enforces unsolicited commercial communication rules. Public contact data is not an invitation or permission to call or message. Initial acquisition in this experiment is primarily relevant, one-to-one **in-person visits and warm introductions**. Do not place unsolicited calls or send unsolicited WhatsApp/SMS. Use phone or messaging only after the owner explicitly invites or schedules that channel, record the permission, stay within its purpose, and stop after an opt-out or two unanswered permission-based follow-ups. Obtain professional advice before any scaled SMS, voice, or WhatsApp campaign.

### WhatsApp

Use the client’s number and client-controlled account. Contact customer prospects only when the client has a valid basis/permission and honours opt-outs. Do not import scraped or purchased lists. Keep the operator’s prospecting data separate from the client’s customer data.

### Google Business Profile

The client retains ownership or co-ownership. Use manager access or client-side upload; never request OTPs. Disclose that the profile is free, document changes, follow media/update policies, and remove access at termination. Do not promise ranking: Google describes local results mainly through relevance, distance, and prominence.

### Privacy and the DPDP transition

The DPDP framework is in phased commencement as of the evidence date. The 2025 Rules and official explanatory material set staged dates, so obligations must be rechecked as they commence. Build to the stricter operating standard now: minimum data, client ownership, purpose limitation, clear notice/permission, access control, retention/deletion, secure transfer, incident contact, and no unnecessary customer-message copies, home addresses, identity documents, floor plans, or health/financial data. Get current legal advice before processing client customer data at scale.

### Property, people, truth, and safety

Obtain written permission before filming private property or identifiable people. Never film minors. Do not expose security-sensitive details. Confirm authorship and credits. Do not fabricate a before/after, remove defects, add features with AI, or imply work the contractor did not perform. Contractor supplies safe access and PPE; no active hazard, ladder, roof, or drone work.

### GST, income tax, TDS, and records

The general GST registration threshold for services is often ₹20 lakh aggregate PAN-wide turnover, with lower thresholds/compulsory cases and state/fact-specific exceptions. Inter-state and e-commerce rules contain exemptions and caveats. Do not rely on a dashboard summary for registration or classification. Ask a CA before voluntary registration, unusual interstate/foreign/platform work, or as turnover approaches the threshold. Keep scopes, invoices, receipts, bank records, expenses, refunds, asset records, access/permission logs, and TDS/AIS/26AS reconciliation. Actual income tax is based on the operator’s full facts and taxable income, not a flat percentage of collections.

### Udyam

Official Udyam registration is free. Do not pay an agent merely to submit the official registration. Registration is not a substitute for tax, local, contract, or sector-specific advice.

- No representative Indian dataset measures contractors buying this exact service.

- The ₹4,900/₹12,000 prices and every sales threshold are test assumptions.
- The 90-day gates are deliberately difficult because survival decisions should not be based on compliments or vanity metrics.
